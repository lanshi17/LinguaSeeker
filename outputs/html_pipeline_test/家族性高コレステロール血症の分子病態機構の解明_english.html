Elucidation of the Molecular Pathogenesis of Familial Hypercholesterolemia

Mika Hori ${ }^{a, b}$

Mechanism of the Molecular Pathophysiology for Familial Hypercholesterolemia

Mika Hori ${ }^{a, b}$

${ }^{a}$ Department of Endocrinology, Research Institute of Environmental Medicine, Nagoya University, Tokai National Higher Education and Research System, Furo-cho, Chikusa-ku, Nagoya 464-8601, Japan; and ${ }^{b}$ Department of Molecular Innovation in Lipidology, National Cerebral and Cardiovascular Center Research Institute, 6-1 Kishibe-shimmachi, Suita, Osaka 564-8565, Japan.

(Received August 20, 2024)

Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is characterized by high serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels from birth, tendon/skin xanthomas, and premature coronary artery disease. The prevalence of FH is 1 per 300 individuals in the general population. FH is caused by a pathogenic (rare) variant in the LDL receptor (LDLR), apolipoprotein B (APOB), and proprotein convertase subtilisin/kinin type 9 (PCS K9) genes. In Japan, there has been only one reported case of a family with FH caused by the known APOB p.(Arg3527Gln) variant. Those without pathogenic variants in the LDLR or PCSK9 genes account for approximately 36% of patients with FH. Novel causative genes/variants of FH have been explored in patients with FH worldwide, but no gene variants with a large effect size have been found. Polygenic hypercholesterolemia accounts for approximately 10% of patients with clinical FH. We performed whole-exome sequencing in 122 families without pathogenic variants in the LDLR and PCSK9 genes. However, we could not find novel causative genes/variants of FH via family analysis. We examined all the APOB variants and showed that the low-frequency APOB p.(Pro955Ser) variant has a moderate effect size in FH patients via functional analysis of hepatocytes. We also reported that low-frequency PCSK9 variants contribute to the severity of the FH phenotype in patients with FH harboring an LDLR pathogenic variant. Thus, the combination of low-frequency variants and age, environmental factors such as diet, or other genetic factors contribute to the severity of or variability in the FH phenotype.

Key words—familial hypercholesterolemia (FH), low-frequency variant, hepatocyte, apolipoprotein B (APOB)

1. Introduction

Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is characterized by high levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in the blood from birth, and features such as thickening of the Achilles tendon and the appearance of raised deposits of cholesterol (xanthomas) on the elbows and arms. Furthermore, due to the accumulation of LDL-C, atherosclerosis progresses, increasing the probability of developing coronary artery disease (angina, myocardial infarction) at a young age. Regarding the relationship between atherosclerosis and LDL-C, the LDL accumulation hypothesis has been proposed, suggesting that there is a threshold for event onset based on the cumulative value of LDL-C level $\times$ years. ${ }^{1)}$ Therefore, managing LDL-C levels is extremely important for the prevention of coronary artery disease. FH is a hereditary disease with an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern, caused by pathogenic variants (genetic changes) with a frequency of less than $1 \%$ (rare) in the population. FH heterozygotes are found at a high frequency of 1 in 300 individuals in the general population in Japan, making it the most common hereditary disease. FH homozygotes have very high LDL-C levels of $370 \mathrm{mg} / \mathrm{dL}$ or more, exist at a frequency of 1 in 360,000 to 1 million people, and are designated as an intractable disease. On the other hand, 20-40% of FH heterozygotes do not have pathogenic variants in the known causative genes, and their cause remains unknown. This article discusses the causative genes of FH and their genetic characteristics in Japan, as well as the identification and functional analysis of new FH causative/associated genes.

2. Causative Genes of FH

In 1973, Dr. Goldstein and Dr. Brown, using experiments with fibroblasts derived from FH homozygous patients, reported that FH is a hereditary disease caused by abnormalities in the LDL receptor (LDLR) gene, which encodes the LDL receptor. 2) To date, over 2000 types of LDLR gene variants have been reported worldwide. 3) Pathogenic variants in the LDLR gene include the defective type, where LDLR activity remains at 2-25%, and the negative type, where activity is less than 2%. Many of the reported variants also have unknown functions. In 1987, the APOB gene, which encodes apolipoprotein B-100 [apolipoprotein B (APOB)-100], the ligand for LDLR, was reported as the second causative gene for FH. 4) The APOB protein has five domains: three amphipathic $\alpha$ -helix domains and two amphipathic $\beta$ -strand domains (NH2-$\beta \alpha 1-$ $\beta 1-\alpha 2-\beta 2-\alpha 3-\mathrm{COOH}$ ), and the $\beta$ -strand domains are thought to contribute to the affinity between lipids and the APOB protein. 5) The known pathogenic p.(Arg3527Gln) variant of the APOB gene was reported in Europe; it reduces the binding affinity between APOB-100 and LDLR but exists outside the LDLR binding site (3359-3369). 4) It is said that the phenotype of FH caused by pathogenic variants in the APOB gene is mild compared to FH caused by pathogenic variants in the LDLR gene. 6) In Japan, we first reported a family with FH caused by p.(Arg3527Gln) in 2020, but the frequency of FH caused by pathogenic variants in the APOB gene is very low. 7) In China, p.(Arg3527Trp) has been reported as a pathogenic variant. 8) As the third causative gene for FH, the proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) gene was identified in 2003 by Abifadel et al. using positional cloning. 9) PCSK9 consists of four domains: a signal peptide, a prodomain, a catalytic domain, and a C-terminal domain. Through autoproteolysis, the cleaved prodomain binds to the catalytic domain, losing its own hydrolytic enzyme activity to become mature PCSK9, which gains LDLR-degrading activity. It has been reported that LDLR recycles to the cell surface about 150 times after taking up LDL from the blood, but LDLR bound to PCSK9 is not recycled and is degraded in lysosomes. One of the pathogenic variants of the PCSK9 gene, the p.(Asp374Trp) variant, greatly reduces LDLR activity and presents an FH phenotype similar in severity to that caused by pathogenic variants in the LDLR gene. This variant is located in the catalytic domain, which is associated with LDLR degradation activity, and it has been experimentally shown to increase LDLR degradation activity (Fig. 1). 10)

3. Genetic Characteristics of FH in Japan

We have been conducting genetic analysis of the LDLR and PCSK9 genes for approximately 1550 patients with FH or suspected FH as part of routine clinical practice at the National Cerebral and Cardiovascular Center (NCVC) in Osaka. 11) Among 650 probands clinically diagnosed as FH heterozygotes, we identified 137 types of variants in the LDLR gene in $57 \%$ $(n=368)$ of the patients. For Mendelian hereditary diseases, the American College of Medical Genetics (ACGM) and the Association for Molecular Pathology (AMP) have established the ACMG guidelines as the global standard for clinical genetic diagnosis. ${ }^{[2]}$ Following these guidelines, we classified 92 variants as pathogenic and 2 variants as non-pathogenic. ${ }^{[3,14]}$ Among the LDLR gene variants, 5 types of pathogenic variants accounted for $30.4 \%$. The breakdown was: $\mathrm{c} .1845+2 \mathrm{T} >\mathrm{C}$ causing splicing errors at exon/intron junctions at $7.5 \%$, amino acid substitutions $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Cys} 338 \mathrm{Ser})$ at $6.4 \%$, $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Lys} 811 *)$ at $5.8 \%$, $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Asp} 433 \mathrm{His})$ at $5.5 \%$, and $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Leu} 568 \mathrm{Val})$ at $5.2 \%$. Although 92 variants were defined as pathogenic, the remaining variants were classified as variants of uncertain significance (VUS), partly due to the small number of families. Therefore, to increase the number of cases and redefine VUS, given that Japanese FH patients are concentrated at NCVC (Osaka) and Kanazawa, we collaborated with Kanazawa University to assess the significance of LDLR gene variants in 472 families and classified 132 variants as pathogenic. ${ }^{[5]}$ At Kanazawa University, the $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Lys} 811 *)$ variant accounted for $41 \%$ of the pathogenic LDLR gene variants, revealing a regional difference between Osaka and Kanazawa. The accumulation of the $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Lys} 811 *)$ variant is thought to be due to a founder effect, where a single variant spreads within a specific population in a limited geographical area, and because Kanazawa has less population influx compared to Osaka, where the National Cerebral and Cardiovascular Center is located.

In $7.8 \%$ $(n=51)$ of FH heterozygote probands, we identified 21 variants in the PCSK9 gene. Following the ACMG guidelines, we classified 4 variants: $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Glu} 32 \mathrm{Lys}), \mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Asp} 129 \mathrm{Asn}), \mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Arg} 215 \mathrm{His}), \mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Arg} 496 \mathrm{Trp})$ as pathogenic (Fig. 1). The $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Glu} 32 \mathrm{Lys})$ variant accounted for $88 \%$ of the detected pathogenic PCSK9 gene variants. ${ }^{[1]}$ Comparing the clinical data of patients with pathogenic variants in the LDLR gene and the PCSK9 gene in Japan, the mean untreated LDL-C level was $275 \pm 73 \mathrm{vs} .201 \pm 42 \mathrm{mg} / \mathrm{dL}$, and the frequency of Achilles tendon thickening was $82 \mathrm{vs} .44 \%$, indicating that the phenotype of pathogenic variants in the PCSK9 gene is mild compared to that of pathogenic variants in the LDLR gene. Therefore, it is difficult to predict the presence of the $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Glu} 32 \mathrm{Lys})$ variant from clinical diagnosis alone. The effect size of the $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Glu} 32 \mathrm{Lys})$ variant on the FH phenotype is smaller compared to the $\mathrm{p} .(\mathrm{Asp} 374 \mathrm{Tyr})$ variant detected overseas.

4. Genetic Approach to FH of Unknown Cause

Among 650 clinically diagnosed probands with heterozygous FH, 64% were definitively diagnosed with FH, while the remaining 36% had no pathogenic variants identified in the LDLR or PCSK9 genes, leaving the genetic factors unknown. Therefore, to elucidate novel causative genes for FH, whole-genome/exome analysis was conducted on families with FH of unknown cause. Whole-genome analysis was performed on a large family with 22 members, which had a strong family history of FH where all seven siblings had experienced myocardial infarction. This analysis identified a novel large-scale duplication variant in the LDLR gene as the cause, including the precise genomic location of the duplication variant. [6] Whole-exome analysis was conducted on 122 Japanese FH families of unknown cause, but no new causative genes were found. Globally, 20-40% of heterozygous FH cases are of unknown cause, and searches for novel causative genes have been conducted, but no variants with large effect sizes have been identified. FH has been considered a monogenic disease caused by rare variants; however, in 2013, a European study reported that FH of unknown cause is polygenic FH, resulting from the accumulation of 12 common single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) across 11 genes (APOE, SORT1, LDLR, APOB, PCSK9, HFE, ABCG8, NYNRIIN, MYLIP, SLC-22, ST3GAL4) contributing to elevated LDL-C levels (with two variants in the APOE gene), [7] and is said to account for 10% of FH cases. Therefore, we focused on the APOB gene, which has a very low frequency in our country's FH. Twenty-four variants were detected, and we noted that the allele frequency of the p.(Pro955Ser) variant was significantly higher in FH patients at 15% compared to 3.4% in the general Japanese population (odds ratio 4.9 [95% CI 3.4-7.1]; p=6.9×10-13). The penetrance of this variant within families was low, and it could not be considered a cause of FH, but patients carrying this variant accounted for 9.8% of heterozygous FH probands (n=645) (Fig. 2). To clarify the function of this variant, LDL was isolated and fluorescently labeled from the blood of an FH proband homozygous for the p.(Pro955Ser) variant and their child heterozygous for the p.(Pro955Ser) variant in that family. As controls, LDL was isolated and fluorescently labeled from the blood of a healthy adult and a proband heterozygous for the known pathogenic p.(Arg3527Gln) variant. 5. Identification of Severe FH

FH homozygotes are either true homozygotes or compound heterozygotes who inherit pathogenic variants in the LDLR gene from both parents. In Japan, it was reported in 2011 that double heterozygous FH, where a pathogenic variant in the LDLR gene overlaps with the PCSK9 gene p.(Glu32Lys) variant, is severe. 20 We reported that the overlap of a pathogenic variant in the LDLR gene with the novel PCSK9 p.(Val4Ile) variant leads to an increase in LDL-C levels and a 40% increased risk of coronary artery disease. 21 We have also reported that the overlap of a pathogenic variant in the LDLR gene with the PCSK9 p.(Glu32Lys) or p.(Val4Ile) variants results in a poor prognosis. 22 These double heterozygous FH cases are also classified as FH homozygotes in clinical diagnosis. The frequencies of the PCSK9 p.(Glu32Lys) and p.(Val4Ile) variants in the general population are 1.1% and 1.3%, respectively, making them low-frequency variants. 11 Tada et al. have reported on oligogenic FH (where a few genes overlap) where the overlap of a pathogenic variant in the LDLR gene with rare variants in the ABCG5, ABCG8, APOE, or LDLRAP1 genes leads to a more severe condition. 23 Recently, a French study reported that the overlap of a pathogenic variant in the LDLR gene with a rare variant in the APOE gene leads to increased LDL-C levels and a higher frequency of coronary artery disease. 24 It is thought that the overlap of a pathogenic variant in the LDLR gene with unknown variants involved in LDL-C elevation may present a homozygous/severe FH phenotype, but sufficient functional analysis has not been conducted. We have found several families who are heterozygous for a pathogenic variant in the LDLR gene but are clinically diagnosed as homozygotes. For these families, we are establishing induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells from patients alongside genomic analysis, generating hepatocytes, and conducting functional evaluations. 25

6. Conclusion

The diversity of FH pathology has long been known, but with the advent of next-generation sequencing enabling comprehensive analysis, various genetic factors defining this diversity have become clearer. Historically, the discovery of novel causative genes for FH led to the development of statins and PCSK9 inhibitors, which have greatly contributed not only to FH treatment but also to the treatment of hyper-LDL-cholesterolemia, impacting global public health. We hope to contribute, however modestly, to understanding the true pathology of FH and hyper-LDL-cholesterolemia and developing new therapeutic targets by appropriately validating the vast genetic information obtained through next-generation sequencing.
