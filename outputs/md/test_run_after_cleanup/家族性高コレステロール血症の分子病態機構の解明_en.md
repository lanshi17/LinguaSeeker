## 1. Introduction  
Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is characterized by elevated serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels from birth, tendon/skin xanthomas, and premature coronary artery disease. Additionally, LDL-C levels remain persistently high despite dietary modifications.  

The relationship between atherosclerosis and LDL-C has been elucidated by the "LDL hypothesis," which states that elevated LDL-C levels directly contribute to the development of atherosclerosis. Over the past century, LDL-C has been recognized as critically important in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis. FH is a hereditary disorder, with an estimated prevalence of 1% among patients with coronary artery disease. The general population prevalence is approximately 1 in 300 individuals.  

FH patients typically exhibit LDL-C levels exceeding 370 mg/dL, with an estimated frequency of 1 in 36–100 million individuals. However, 20–40% of FH patients do not meet the diagnostic criteria for heterozygous FH. In this review, we discuss the molecular characteristics of FH and recent advances in the identification of novel FH-related genes through genetic analysis.  

## 2. Causative Genes of FH  
In 1973, Goldstein and Brown identified the LDL receptor (LDLR) gene as the causative gene for FH. To date, over 2,000 LDLR variants have been reported worldwide. LDLR mutations can be classified into defective types (residual function of 2–25%) and negative types (residual function <2%), with many variants of uncertain significance remaining uncharacterized.  

In 1987, the second causative gene for FH, apolipoprotein B (APOB), was identified. APOB encodes the ligand for LDLR, apolipoprotein B-100 (APOB-100). APOB consists of five domains: three amphipathic α-helical domains and two amphipathic β-sheet domains (NH2-βα1-β1-α2-β2-α3-COOH). The β-sheet domains are critical for binding to LDLR. The most common APOB variant, p.(Arg3527Gln), reduces the binding affinity of APOB-100 to LDLR but does not affect other LDLR-binding regions (residues 3359–3369). FH caused by APOB mutations generally presents with milder cardiovascular manifestations compared to LDLR-related FH.  

In Japan, only one family with FH caused by the APOB p.(Arg3527Gln) variant has been reported, indicating its rarity. Other APOB variants, such as p.(Arg3527Trp), have also been identified.  

In 2003, Abifadel et al. identified proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) as the third causative gene for FH through linkage analysis. PCSK9 consists of four domains: a prodomain, catalytic domain, hinge region, and C-terminal domain. Gain-of-function PCSK9 variants increase LDLR degradation, reducing LDLR recycling to the cell surface. Normally, LDLR undergoes ~150 cycles of endocytosis and recycling, but PCSK9-bound LDLR is degraded in lysosomes instead. One notable PCSK9 variant is p.(Asp374Tyr).

## 3. Genetic Characteristics of FH in Japan  
In Japan, genetic testing was performed on 1,550 clinically diagnosed **FH** or suspected **FH** patients, identifying pathogenic variants in **LDLR** and **PCSK9**. Among 650 probands meeting **FH** diagnostic criteria, **LDLR** variants were detected in 57% (n=368), comprising 137 distinct variants (Fig. 1). Using **ACMG**/**AMP** guidelines, 92 variants were classified as pathogenic/likely pathogenic, while 2 were benign.

- Five recurrent variants accounted for 30.4% of cases:  
  - c.1845+2T>C (7.5%)  
  - p.(Cys338Ser) (6.4%)

  - p.(Leu568Val) (5.2%)
