## 1. Introduction  
Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is characterized by elevated serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels from birth, tendon/skin xanthomas, and premature coronary artery disease. Additionally, LDL-C levels remain persistently high despite dietary modifications.  

The relationship between atherosclerosis and LDL-C has been explained by the "LDL retention hypothesis," which posits that the accumulation of LDL-C in arterial walls contributes to atherosclerotic plaque formation. Over the past century, LDL-C has been recognized as critically important in atherosclerosis. FH is an autosomal dominant disorder, with a prevalence estimated at 1% in the general population. The global prevalence of FH is approximately 1 in 300 individuals.  

FH patients typically exhibit LDL-C levels exceeding 370 mg/dL, with diagnostic criteria suggesting a prevalence of 1 in 36–100 individuals. However, 20–40% of FH patients do not meet the diagnostic criteria, highlighting the need for further investigation into the molecular mechanisms and novel genetic factors underlying FH.  

## 2. Causative Genes of FH  
In 1973, Goldstein and Brown identified the LDL receptor (*LDLR*) gene as the causative gene for FH. To date, over 2,000 *LDLR* variants have been reported worldwide. *LDLR* pathogenic variants can be classified into defective-type (2–25% residual function) and negative-type (<2% residual function), though many variants remain functionally uncharacterized.  

In 1987, the *APOB* gene was identified as the second causative gene for FH. *APOB* encodes apolipoprotein B-100 (*APOB*-100), a ligand for the LDLR. *APOB* consists of five domains: three amphipathic α-helical domains and two amphipathic β-sheet domains (NH2-βα1-β1-α2-β2-α3-COOH). The β-sheet domains are critical for *APOB*-100 binding to the LDLR. The *APOB* p.(Arg3527Gln) variant reduces the binding affinity of *APOB*-100 to the LDLR but does not affect other LDLR-binding regions (residues 3359–3369). FH caused by *APOB* variants generally presents with milder cardiovascular manifestations compared to *LDLR*-related FH.  

In Japan, only one family with FH caused by the *APOB* p.(Arg3527Gln) variant has been reported, indicating its rarity. Other *APOB* variants, such as p.(Arg3527Trp), have also been identified.  

In 2003, Abifadel et al. identified *PCSK9* as the third causative gene for FH using a candidate gene approach. *PCSK9* consists of four domains: a prodomain, catalytic domain, hinge region, and C-terminal domain. Gain-of-function *PCSK9* variants enhance *PCSK9*-mediated degradation of the LDLR, reducing LDLR recycling to the cell surface. Normally, the LDLR undergoes ~150 cycles of endocytosis, but *PCSK9* binding prevents recycling, leading to lysosomal degradation. The *PCSK9* p.(Asp374Tyr) variant is one such gain-of-function variant.
