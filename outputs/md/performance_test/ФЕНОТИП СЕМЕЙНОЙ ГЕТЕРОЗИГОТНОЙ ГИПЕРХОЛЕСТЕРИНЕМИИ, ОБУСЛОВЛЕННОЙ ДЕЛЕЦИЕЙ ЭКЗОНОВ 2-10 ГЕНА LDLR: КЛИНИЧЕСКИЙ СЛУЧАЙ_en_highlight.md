# Phenotype of heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia caused by deletion of LDLR gene exons 2-10: a case report  

**Moiseeva A.M.**¹, **Ivanova O.N.**², **Emelyanchik V.S.**¹, **Emelyanchik E.Yu.**¹, **Marilovtseva O.V.**¹, **Nikulina S.Yu.**¹, **Protopopov A.V.**¹  

## Introduction  
Analysis and comparison of clinical and functional characteristics of patients with familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) with molecular genetics data make it possible to clarify the disease nature and determine the management strategy taking into account the clinical and genetic status. Considering that biochemical criteria are the leading ones in the diagnosis of FH, the study of family cases complements the disease understanding.  

## Brief description  
An analysis of the genealogical and individual data, physical and general clinical examination of three family members, advanced lipid testing, and vascular ultrasound were performed. A two-stage molecular genetics study was conducted as follows: the first stage involved parallel sequencing of 60 genes associated with FH; the second stage involved DNA assessment by the multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification method of the low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) gene to identify large deletions.  

## Discussion  
Patients with deletions in LDLR gene exons 2-10 have the most severe clinical and laboratory abnormalities and early development of cardiovascular diseases. Families with FH have the highest concentration of genomic abnormalities that can lead to atherosclerosis lesions even in preschool children and may require non-standard lipid-lowering therapy, including biopharmaceuticals.

Heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) has a higher prevalence in the Russian population compared to European populations, reaching 1:173 [1]. In countries without universal and/or targeted screening, the detection of patients with FH does not exceed 1% of all patients [2]. This makes the disease "invisible" to most outpatient physicians, especially in patients under 40 years of age; as a result, FH is diagnosed at the stage of acute coronary artery disease (CAD) development. This is particularly evident in young people with FH and subclinical course of atherosclerotic diseases [3].  

Given the high cardiovascular risk associated with prolonged exposure to high concentrations of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), stratification of cardiovascular risk in young patients requires analysis of the correlation between clinical symptoms of FH and the genetic architecture of the disease [1, 3].

### FH. Functional Role of the LDL Receptor  
The LDL receptor's functional role involves capturing and binding particles on the surface of liver cells, followed by their internalization for utilization [4]. The Human Gene Mutation Database Professional 2022.1 lists 2,331 unique pathogenic variants in LDLR, of which 18% are large rearrangements. The primary cause of these rearrangements is the relatively high frequency of Alu repeats within the LDLR gene—2.2/1 kbp.  

The autosomal dominant inheritance pattern of this disease implies its highest prevalence among hereditary disorders, clinical manifestations in both sexes, and involvement of 50% of offspring in each family generation. Since the hallmark of familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is early-onset atherosclerotic vascular damage, correlating clinical features with genotype can determine its penetrance and severity [5, 6]. Accordingly, the leading contribution of cardiovascular diseases (CVD) to mortality rates in Russia drives the search for novel pathogenic LDLR variants across different regions [7].  

We present a clinical case study of three members of Family X diagnosed with FH before age 40. Among the proband’s first- and second-degree relatives, a high concentration of patients with early-onset coronary artery disease (CAD), recurrent (including fatal) coronary events was observed, despite treatment in interventional cardiology hospitals (Fig. 1).  

The aim of this study is to analyze phenotypic disease features across two generations of an FH-affected family and correlate clinical data with structural genomic variants in patients.
