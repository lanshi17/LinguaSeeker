# Functional Characterization of TRPV1 Channel Variants

## Abstract

This study investigates the functional properties of wild-type and mutant TRPV1 channels using electrophysiology and calcium imaging techniques.

## Introduction

The TRPV1 channel is a polymodal nociceptor that plays a critical role in pain sensation and thermoregulation. As a member of the transient receptor potential (TRP) family, it can be activated by various stimuli including capsaicin, heat (>43°C), and protons.

## Methods

### Cell Culture and Transfection

HEK293 cells were transiently transfected with wild-type TRPV1 cDNA using Lipofectamine 2000 according to manufacturer's instructions. Cells were maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS at 37°C in 5% CO₂.

### Experimental Controls

All experiments were performed with n = 3 biological replicates and repeated at least three times independently. Statistical significance was determined using one-way ANOVA with post-hoc Tukey test.

## Results

### Dose-Response Analysis

Capsaicin evoked robust calcium influx in TRPV1-expressing cells with EC50 of 44.2 ± 3.1 nM, consistent with published literature values. The Hill coefficient was calculated to be 1.8 ± 0.2, indicating positive cooperativity.
