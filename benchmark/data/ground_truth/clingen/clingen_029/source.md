## Abstract

Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH) is a rare, progressive disorder characterized by pathological vascular remodeling of the pulmonary arteries, ultimately leading to right heart failure. Early diagnosis remains challenging, as clinical manifestations are often nonspecific, and definitive confirmation still requires invasive right heart catheterization. Circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as promising biomarkers for cardiovascular diseases due to their plasma stability and direct involvement in disease pathophysiology. We performed small RNA sequencing on plasma samples from 25 IPAH patients and 10 healthy controls to identify differentially expressed miRNAs. Seven candidate miRNAs were selected for further validation by quantitative PCR in a multicenter cohort of 110 IPAH patients and controls. Logistic regression models were built to evaluate diagnostic performance. Functional studies for miR-3168 were performed using western blot and flow cytometry in Pulmonary Artery Endothelial Cells (PAECs), and tube formation assay in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs). Our initial screen identified 29 differentially expressed miRNAs. Seven of these candidates, including members of the let-7 family and miR-3168, were successfully validated in the larger cohort. A diagnostic panel incorporating three miRNAs (let-7a-5p, miR-9-5p, and miR-31-5p) was developed, which achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.862 (95% CI = 0.7481–1) for discriminating PAH patients from controls. Separately, we investigated the functional role of miR-3168, which was upregulated in PAH patients. In PAECs, overexpression of miR-3168 led to a reduction of BMPR2 protein levels. Moreover, miR-3168 overexpression impaired tube formation in HUVECs. Our study identifies a plasma-derived three-miRNA signature with strong potential for the non-invasive diagnosis of PAH. Furthermore, we implicate miR-3168 as a potential contributor to endothelial dysfunction through its regulation of BMPR2 and its anti-angiogenic effects in vitro. These findings reinforce the dual utility of circulating miRNAs as both clinically relevant non-invasive biomarkers and as tools to discover novel disease biology. ## Background

PAH is a rare and devastating disease characterized by the thickening of the precapillary pulmonary arteries. This induces an increase in pulmonary vascular resistance that eventually results in right heart failure 1 . The molecular basis of PAH is complex, genetics play a big role on it as the heritable (HPAH) and some idiopathic (IPAH) cases can be explained in a high percentage by mutations in an ever-expanding set of genes 2 .

PAH early symptoms include shortness of breath, fatigue, weakness, angina and syncope, which are common for a wide array of disorders 3 . This complicates early diagnosis, often leading to delays as confirmation requires right heart catheterization (RHC) 4 . An early diagnosis and treatment is crucial to improve survival and quality of life during disease progression 5 .

Several initiatives have been undertaken to enhance the diagnosis and classification of PAH using various -omic tools in plasma and serum. However, the only biomarker currently recommended for predicting prognosis in PAH is NT-proBNP 3 , which is a marker of heart failure that correlates well with right heart dysfunction 6 . The quest for novel diagnostic markers began with the study of the metabolome to phenotype IPAH/HPAH against healthy donor controls. This approach successfully identified differences between the groups and also predicted the risk of early mortality and response to treatment 7 . Similarly, proteomic profiling aided in the classification of different PAH phenotypes based on variations in immune proteins 8 , or even for early diagnosis of PAH associated with systemic sclerosis 9 . More recently, RNA sequencing in whole blood also demonstrated its potential in distinguishing PAH and predicting prognosis 10 .

MiRNA are small non-coding RNAs of around 20–22 bp that regulate gene expression through binding to the 3’untranslated regions of genes 11 . They can be found in plasma circulating bound to Ago2 proteins, inside apoptotic bodies, and vesicles of different types. The diagnostic potential of circulating miRNAs has been proposed for years across a wide variety of diseases. PAH is no exception to this trend. In the initial approach, a miRNA array was used to compare lung tissues from individuals with corrected septal defects who did not develop PAH to those who did. This analysis identified miR-19a as a potential marker for PAH 12 . Not much later, researchers worked with an array to test miRNA levels in plasma samples taken right after the RHC. They found that miR-663 was a good classifier of response to O 2 and iNO challenges 13 . In a different way, circulating miRNA have also been tested in the right ventricle and the pulmonary circulation, finding differences depending on where the samples were taken 14 . However, the latest advancement has been the development of a miRNA signature for PAH diagnosis using treatment-naïve patients. This approach ultimately identified 2 novel miRNAs (miR-187 and miR-636) with a clear association with PAH 15 .

In this article, we utilized microRNA-seq to examine the differences in circulating miRNA levels between idiopathic PAH patients mostly in functional classes-NYHA I and II and healthy controls. Our objectives were to identify the hallmarks of these early functional classes and discover novel miRNAs implicated in the disease.


## Methods


## Description of the cohort

For the RNA-seq analysis, we selected 10 patients from the Vigo area and 15 from Spanish PAH registry (REHAP) biobank (hosted at IDIBAPS), ideally classified as NYHA-functional classes I or II. The 10 healthy donor’s plasma samples were kindly provided by LM-R from Instituto de Investigación Sanitaria de Santiago de Compostela, consisting of age- and sex-matched healthy volunteers with no history of cardiovascular, respiratory, or other chronic systemic diseases. All control subjects were confirmed non-smokers and were not on chronic medications.. All diagnostic procedures followed the recommendations of the ESC/ERS guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of PAH 1 . Upon suspicion of PAH, RHC was performed to confirm the diagnosis.

For qPCR validation, all 110 patient samples were obtained from IDIBAPS and the 110 control samples were provided by the Spanish Biobank Network, samples were matched for age and sex; however, individual demographic data for the controls were not accessible. We split this cohort randomly in two subcohorts: prevalidation cohort (50 patients and 50 healthy donors) and validation (60 patients and 60 healthy donors), this split allowed us to rule out miRNAs whose levels were too low or too variable out of the 13 we chose initially. The study was conducted after the approval of the Comité de ética da Investigación de Galicia (CEIC Galicia) in accordance the clinical-ethical practices of the Spanish Government and the Helsinki Declaration.

An overview of the study design is depicted in Fig. 1 A. Fig. 1 Schematic representation of the study.


## RNA extraction and microRNA-sequencing

We centrifuged the plasma samples at 15.000 × g for 15 min to get rid of cellular debris. Then, we isolated 450 µl in a new tube and proceeded to microRNA extraction using the miRNeasy Advanced Serum/Plasma kit (Qiagen) following manufacturer’s protocol. After extraction, we quantified the miRNAs using the Qubit MicroRNA assay (ThermoFisher) and sent 10 ng for library preparation.

Libraries were prepared by RealSeq Biosciences (California, USA) using an in-house protocol of an Illumina Small RNA-like kit. Quality control was performed for all the libraries before sequencing, a total of 10 million single-end reads were expected per sample. Samples were analyzed in an Illumina—NextSeq 500 v2 High-Output—SR 75 Cycle (Illumina).

Reads were trimmed using CutAdapt 2.3 and aligned to miRBase with bowtie 16 , 17 . Then, we used Samtools 1.7 to convert SAM files to BAM, we built an index using Picard to convert the BAMs to read files, and used FeatureCounts (from the RSubread package) to obtain the count matrix for each miRNA 18 . Finally, we carried out the differential expression analysis using DESeq2 and EdgeR following the recommended protocol with a likelihood-ratio test to obtain the adjusted p-values 19 , 20 . The results between the two methods were compared and those miRNAs common between them were considered for the next steps. All the plots were generated using the ggplot2 package in R 21 . All sequencing data can be accessed in GEO ( GSE222022 ).


## cDNA synthesis and qPCR

After elution of the miRNAs, we used 2 µl of the freshly extracted miRNAs to obtain cDNA using the Applied Biosystems TaqMan Advanced miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit (ThermoFisher) following manufacturer’s protocol. The kit adds a poly-A tail to all the miRNAs, ligates an adapter and performs a RT. After obtaining the cDNA, we did a preamplification of all the miRNA + adaptor complexes using the Taq and universal primers provided by the kit. Finally, we diluted 1:10 the PCR products in nuclease free water. For the qPCR we used the TaqMan Advanced miRNA Assay (A25576, ThermoFisher), the reaction was carried out in a StepOne Plus Real Time PCR system (ThermoFisher) using the fast setup recommended by the TaqMan Fast Advanced Master Mix (ThermoFisher). The cycling conditions were: Initial enzyme activation at 95 °C for 20 s, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 1 s and an anneal and extend step at 60 °C for 20 s. All miRNA probe codes are shown in Supplementary Table 1 .

Data were analyzed using the 2 -ΔΔCt using the geometric mean of miR-103a-3p and miR-16-5p as reference miRNAs 22 – 24 . These reference miRNAs were selected using the data from the RNA-seq, they showed high count numbers, low FC difference between patients and controls (log2FC > -0.2 and < 0.2 with no statistically significant difference), and low variability. We then applied a log 2 transformation to the normalized values before applying the classification models. To plot the individual miRNA levels, we did a Fold Change (FC) comparing patients against controls.


## Classification models and statistics

All the statistical analyses were performed in R (version 3.6). During the prevalidation, we performed a logistic regression using the miRNAs amplified by qPCR. Then, we used a Lasso regression to limit the number of variables in the predictive model. Based on the coefficients estimated in the Lasso, we generated models reducing the miRNA variables one at a time. The formulas to calculate the risk of PAH were built with these coefficients. To estimate the predictive ability of the models we carried out a Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis using 1000 bootstrap replicates with the “pROC" R package to obtain the AUC 25 . In the validation stage, we tested the model with 50 different patients and controls. Then, we reformulated the model using 70% of all samples, leaving 30% to analyze the ROC curves and estimate sensitivity/specificity.


## Risk stratification and correlation analysis

Using the clinical data from the prevalidation and validation cohorts, we calculated the risk score following the criteria described in the COMPERA study 26 . Briefly, we used the NYHA functional class at diagnosis, 6-min walking distance, NT-proBNP or BNP, cardiac index, and right atrial pressure. We assigned risk categories according to the predefined cut-offs and visualized the resulting correlations using the R package “corrplot” ( https://github.com/taiyun/corrplot ). Pairwise Spearman correlations between clinical parameters and miRNA expression were computed after merging hemodynamic and miRNA data by patient identifier, and only statistically significant coefficients were displayed in the correlograms. Patients lacking miRNA amplification (n = 7) or complete hemodynamic information (n = 1) were excluded. Correlation coefficients were computed using Pearson’s method on the numeric variables, and only patients with complete paired data were included. Correlation matrices and significance levels were visualized using “corrplot”.


## Target prediction and functional assays

We used miRBase to predict potential targets of the dysregulated miRNAs we identified 27 . While the three-miRNA panel demonstrated strong diagnostic utility, we sought to investigate a candidate with potential for novel mechanistic insights. From our initial validated list of seven miRNAs, we selected miR-3168 for functional analysis due to its significant upregulation, relative novelty in the context of PAH, and compelling bioinformatic predictions linking it to the core BMPR2 signaling pathway. All predictions can be found in the Supporting Data miRNA predictions.


## qPCR and western-blot

We transfected HeLa cells with 20 µM/40 µM of miR-3168 mimic (#HMI1108, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) or the scrambled miR (#MCH00000, Applied Biological Materials, Canada) using Lipofectamine RNAiMax (ThermoFisher). After 48 h, we extracted mRNA with the NZY total mRNA isolation Kit (NZYtech) and performed a RT-PCR using the NZY M-MuLV First-Strand cDNA synthesis kit (NZYtech). Then, we diluted the 1:10 the cDNA and carried out a qPCR using Power-up SYBR green (ThermoFisher) and the following primers: BMPR2 (forward 5’-AGAGACCCAAGTTCCCAGAAGC-3’, reverse 5’-CCTTTCCTCAGCACACTGTGCA), HDAC2 (forward 5’- CTCATGCACCTGGTGTCCAGAT-3’, reverse 5’- GCTATCCGCTTGTCTGATGCTC-3’), ALAS1 (forward 5’- AGTGTGAAAACCGATGGAGG-3’, reverse 5’- CGATCATACTGAAAAGTGGAAACAG-3’), YWHAZ (forward 5’- ATGCAACCAACACATCCTATC-3’, reverse 5’- GCATTATTAGCGTGCTGTCTT-3’). We normalized the expression using the ddCT method with YWHAZ and ALAS1 as reference genes.

We plated PAECs cells in a 6-well plate. After achieving an 80–90% confluence, we transfected them with a 40 µM concentration of miR-3168, scramble or miR-3168 + antago-miR-3168 (IDT). After 48 h, we extracted the protein using RIPA buffer and quantified it using Pierce BCA protein Kit (ThermoFisher).

We prepared 20 µg of protein in Laemlii’s sample buffer (BioRad) containing 5% B-mercaptoethanol (Sigma) and heated it at 95 °C for 5 min. Proteins were then separated by SDS-Page using a 7.5% mini-Protean TGX precast gel (BioRad). After electrophoresis, we transferred the proteins into a PVDF membrane using a Trans-Blot Turbo Transfer pack (BioRad) in a Trans-Blot Turbo system (BioRad) for 7 min at 1.3 A. We then incubated the membrane for 1 h in a blocking buffer composed of 5% w/v nonfat milk in Tris-buffered saline (TBS) containing 0.2% Tween20 (TBS-T). Immunoblotting was carried out by incubating the membrane with a 1:1000 dilution of anti-BMPR2 antibody (Abcam #ab130206) for 1 h at RT 28 . Then, we washed the membrane three times in blocking buffer and incubated it with a 1:10,000 dilution of anti-Mouse IgG-HRP antibody (Abcam, #205,719). Finally, we washed the membrane with TBS and developed the blot using Clarity Western ECL substrate (BioRad). We stained the membrane with Coomassie Brilliant Blue and used total protein as loading control 29 . Imaging was carried out in a ChemiDoc (BioRad) digital camera-based imaging system.


## Flow cytometry

We transfected PAECs in passages 3–5 with: miR-3168, scramble or miR-3168 + antago-miR-3168 at 40 µM concentration. After 24 h, we changed the media for EGM supplemented with 2% FBS for 24 h. Then, we harvested the cells and the media, pelleted them, and washed them twice in PBS without Calcium. Finally, we resuspended the pellets with Annexin binding buffer containing an anti-Annexin-FITC and 7-AAD (FITC Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit with 7-AAD, BioLegend). We incubated the mixes for 15 min and run the cytometry using a Cytoflex (Beckman Coulter). Data analysis was performed using Cytexpert (Beckman Coulter).


## Tube formation analysis

After transfecting PAECs in the same conditions as before, we changed the media after 24 h for EGM supplemented with 0.2% FBS. After 24 h, we trypsinized the cells, counted them and plated in full growth media 20.000 cells per well in 96-well plates pre-coated with Matrigel (Sigma). We imaged the wells after 12 h using a Nikon TiU inverted microscope. Brightfield images were then analyzed using the Angiogenesis analyzer ImageJ plugin 30 .


## Results


## Discovery cohort description

To this end, we prospectively collected plasma samples from 25 patients recently diagnosed with Idiopathic Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (IPAH), with a focus on including individuals in lower NYHA Functional Classes (I-III). This patient group was compared to 10 healthy donors, well-matched for age and sex.

The clinical characteristics of this discovery cohort are detailed in Table 1 . The patients exhibited a range of disease severity at diagnosis, and most were on mono- or dual-therapy at the time of sampling, consistent with an early-to-intermediate disease population. This intentional study design aimed to maximize the potential for discovering miRNAs altered early in the disease process, which may have greater utility as diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers. ## Differential expression analysis

We identified 29 differentially expressed miRNAs between PAH patients and healthy controls using DESeq2, applying a false discovery rate (FDR) threshold of < 1% and a fold change (FC) cutoff of ± 50% (Fig. 2 A). Principal component analysis revealed clustering of PAH samples with high inter-donor variability (Fig. 2 B). We repeated the analysis using EdgeR and cross-compared results. Of the 29 miRNAs, 26 were consistently identified across both tools with similar fold changes and statistical significance (Supplementary Fig. 1 ). ## MiR-9-5p, miR-31-5p and miR-3168 are upregulated in PAH

MiR-9-5p, miR-31-5p, and miR-3168 were significantly upregulated in PAH patients (Figs. 3 E-G). Notably, miR-9-5p has been associated with cell proliferation and apoptosis resistance in different types of cancers and is predicted to bind to ATP13A3 , one of the newest PAH causal genes 31 , 32 .


## Diagnostic classification models

To assess diagnostic potential, we performed Lasso regression to prioritize the most informative miRNAs (Table 3 ). MiR-9-5p and let-7a-5p showed the strongest contributions. Using the 5 miRNA as predictors, we generated a logistic regression model which achieved an AUC of 0.888 (95% CI = 0.7215–1) (Fig. 3 H, orange line). Subsequently, we used an iterative exclusion approach, building models with decreasing numbers of miRNAs. The three-miRNA panel (miR-9-5p, miR-31-5p, let-7a-5p) was selected as the final model, as it retained high diagnostic accuracy with an AUC of 0.878 (95% CI = 0.5801–0.8376) (Fig. 3 H, blue line), while dropping to a two-miRNA model resulted in a significant decrease in discriminatory power.Based on this, we proceeded with these three miRNAs for full-cohort validation. ## Biomarker validation

We quantified the expression of let-7a-5p, miR-9-5p, and miR-31-5p in an independent cohort of 60 PAH patients and 70 controls. After normalization with miR-16-5p and miR-103a-3p; miR-9-5p and miR-31-5p remained significantly elevated in PAH patients, while let-7a-5p lost statistical significance (Figs. 4 A–C). When we applied the pre-validation model to this larger cohort, diagnostic performance declined (AUC = 0.736, 95% CI = 0.54–0.8149), reflecting reduced specificity/sensitivity (Fig. 4 D). ## Panel validation

We used the model generated during the prevalidation and applied it to the validation set. As expected, after plotting the levels of the miRNAs, the classification worsened obtaining an AUC of 0.736 (95% CI = 0.54–0.8149) with deteriorated cut points (Fig. 4 D).


## Risk analysis and correlations with miRNA levels

To explore the potential prognostic value of the miRNA panel, we calculated clinical risk scores using the COMPERA method and stratified miRNA levels by WHO functional class. Let-7a-5p was the only miRNA with levels that varied by functional class, showing increased expression in classes II and III (Supplementary Fig. 4 A–C). However, no strong correlations were found between individual miRNAs and the overall risk score (Supplementary Figs. 4 D and 5 ).

Correlation analyses with clinical and hemodynamic variables revealed a weak association between let-7a-5p and age at diagnosis, and a negative correlation between miR-3168 and systemic oxygen saturation (Supplementary Fig. 6 ).


## Target prediction and functional analysis of miR-3168

We prioritized predicted targets of miR-3168 with relevance to PAH and high confidence scores (> 80; Supporting Data miRNA predictions). Notably, miR-3168 was predicted to bind two sites within the BMPR2 3′UTR and also targeted HDAC2 (Supplementary Fig. 8 and 9 ). We transfected cells with miR-3168 mimics at 20 µM and 40 µM and observed a dose-dependent reduction in mRNA levels of BMPR2 and HDAC2. BMPR2 levels were reduced to 0.745 ± 0.167 at 20 µM and to 0.406 ± 0.135 at 40 µM. HDAC2 mRNA levels were also reduced (0.579 ± 0.11 at 20 µM and 0.589 ± 0.27 at 40 µM) (Supplementary Fig. 9 ).

At the protein level, BMPR2 was significantly decreased after treatment with 40 µM miR-3168 (0.25 ± 0.11 vs scramble control = 1.0 ± 0.22, p < 0.05). ## Cell viability and apoptosis

We detected no significant differences in cell viability or apoptosis between miR-3168-treated and control groups in HUVECs (n = 7; Supplementary Fig. 10 ).


## Angiogenesis assays

We assessed the functional effects of miR-3168 on angiogenesis using tube formation assays in PAECs. Treatment with miR-3168 significantly reduced total tube number (− 33.8%, p < 0.01), tube length (− 28%, p < 0.001), and number of branches (− 15%, p > 0.05) (Figs. 5 B–E). However, antago-miR-3168 only partially rescued tube formation, reducing the loss in tube length to 14.5%. While co-transfection with the miR-3168 inhibitor successfully rescued BMPR2 protein levels, it did not lead to a statistically significant restoration of tube formation. Suggesting that the anti-angiogenic effect of miR-3168 in this assay may be mediated by mechanisms potentially involving other target genes in addition to BMPR2 .


## Discussion

Early diagnosis of PAH remains a significant challenge. In most cases, patients are diagnosed when the pulmonary vasculature is about to collapse. We have known for several years that the sooner the treatments start, the better the prognosis. We are not only speaking of life expectancy, but also quality of life. That’s why we urgently need diagnostic tools that can be used before patients exhibit symptoms. Furthermore, the diagnosis of PAH must always be confirmed through right heart catheterization, an invasive procedure that, like any intervention, carries inherent risks.

In this article we aimed to characterize the circulating miRNome of IPAH patients in early functional classes, discover novel miRNAs that could be involved in the pathogenesis and develop an efficient diagnostic signature.

We were able to identify 26 miRNAs that were DE between IPAH and controls. Then, from these 26 we limited to 7 the number of candidates for the classification models and used in silico predictions to discover interesting target genes (Supporting Data miRNA predictions).

In our DE analysis we detected the let-7 family members’ let-7a, let-7b, let-7c and let-7f. to be downregulated in the patients. This was not the first time that members of this family were related to PAH, as let-7a-5p had been reported to be downregulated under hypoxia and let-7f.-5p after monocrotaline treatment in a rat model 33 , while the closely related let-7i-5p was upregulated in IPAH patients 34 . From this cluster, let-7b-5p and let-7c-5p did not show statistically significant downregulation in our prevalidation cohort, while let-7a-5p and let-7f.-5p did so. However, in the validation set let-7a-5p also lost its significance. The miRNAs of the let-7 family are known repressors of cell cycle progression and proliferation 35 . They are well characterized tumor repressors, and their downregulation is associated with worse prognosis in different types of cancers 36 – 38 . If we couple this data with the fact that let-7 alter the levels of ET-1 by binding to the 3’UTR of EDN1 39 , 40 , we can hypothesize that a downregulation of this miRNAs could play a role in the development of PAH. An increase of vasoconstriction via ET-1 production and increased PAEC proliferation due to altered cell cycle would fit very well with PAH development.

In the case of the upregulated miRNAs, we had many more candidates, but the number of copies we detected in the sequencing was very variable, with some individuals having thousands of copies and others none. Thus, we were only able to amplify consistently in all samples miR-9-5p, miR-31-5p and miR-3168. All of them were overexpressed in PAH patients in the prevalidation cohort, and the prioritized miRNAs (miR-9-5p and miR-31-5p) were also overexpressed in the full cohort, although the differences in the validation cohort alone were lower.

The overexpression of miR-9-5p has been reported in different cancer types. It has been well characterized in glioma, where miR-9-5p is described to increase proliferation, migration and invasion of the cancer cells 41 . They also secrete miR-9-5p the exosomes to increase angiogenesis, something that was proved in vitro by the increase of HUVEC proliferation and tube formation after treating them with glioma-derived exosomes 41 . This promotion of angiogenesis happens due to miR-9 inhibition of S1P 1 42 . But not only endothelial cells can be influenced by this miRNA, fibroblasts can be involved in a crosstalk with tumors, increasing its motility and enhancing epithelial-mesenchymal transition 43 , 44 . Altogether, miR-9-5p could be promoting PAEC proliferation and helping in the development of PAH.

High levels of miR-31-5p have been associated with tumor progression in several types of cancers 45 – 47 . There are several circular RNAs that act as a sponge for this miRNA, when their levels are low the prognosis is worse 48 , 49 . The pathways regulated by miR-31-5p are diverse, but in vitro experiments have shown that it enhances proliferation, migration and inhibited apoptosis 48 . But miR-31-5p has not only been reported in cancer, in aortic dissection/aneurism miR-31-5p expression downregulated the expression of contractile genes by lowering myocardin levels 50 . Although, miR-31-5p is also supposed to lower inflammation by downregulating Protein Kinase C ε and indirectly the NF-κB pathway 51 . Recently, miR-31-5p was reported to be upregulated in the right ventricle of PAH patients, especially in male patients, where its expression was regulated by a network of circular RNAs and lncRNAs 52 . Besides, miR-31-5p could target ATP13A3 , whose haploinsufficiency causes PAH 31 , 32 . Thus, the implication of this miRNA in PAH pathogenesis make sense if we focus in proliferation/inhibition and how it is reported to interact with vascular smooth muscle cells (SMCs) 48 , 50 , it could help in the overall proliferative environment of PAH-development with enhanced SMC/adventitial fibroblast proliferation and migration. However, the inhibition of inflammatory responses may be protective effect in this case.

MiR-3168 is the least known miRNA that we studied, some of the targets predicted for it included several BMP receptors involved in PAH ( BMPR2 and BMPR1A ) 2 . When we analyzed the BMPR2 mRNA, we found that it decreases after treating the cells with miR-3168, the same happened with HDAC2 levels (another of the miRDB-predicted targets, Supplementary Fig. 9 ; Supporting Data miRNA predictions ). The protein levels of BMPR2 were also downregulated. Furthermore, BMPR2 inhibition is one of the better understood causes of endothelial dysfunction 53 – 55 , as it increases PAEC apoptosis and impairs angiogenesis 56 , 57 , Nevertheless, HDAC2 is upregulated in pulmonary adventitial fibroblasts from IPAH patients and it is mostly expressed in SMCs 58 . Our functional studies of miR-3168 pointed out that it alters angiogenesis in vitro, which supports our finding of it targeting BMPR2 but the relationship between this miRNA and PAH should be further studied. Regarding the biological source of this miRNA, we used the Endothelial Lung Atlas and found that miR-3168 was only detected in type 2 conventional dendritic cells (DCs), with no reads detected in any other cell type 59 . We could speculate the activated DCs might produce increased amounts of miR-3168 and contribute to endothelial dysfunction just like monocyte-derived DCs have been shown to do 60 .

Our miRNA extraction discarded big exosomes and apoptotic bodies with the first centrifugation, but we have extracted miRNAs from smaller vesicles. Proof of this is that a big part of let-7a-5p is linked to vesicles 61 , so we cannot be sure whether or not this miRNA dysregulation is showing crosstalk between PAH-specific cellular types.

Our study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, regarding our mechanistic findings, we demonstrate that miR-3168 reduces BMPR2 mRNA and protein levels. We further confirmed the specificity of this effect at the protein level, as co-transfection with a miR-3168 antagomir successfully reversed the reduction in BMPR2. However, because we did not validate a direct binding interaction using a luciferase reporter assay with the BMPR2 3’UTR. The link presented here should be considered a strong, functionally supported hypothesis that provides a compelling foundation for future mechanistic studies. Second, while most of our functional assays were performed in disease-relevant PAECs, the tube formation assay was conducted in HUVECs. Although HUVECs are a standard model for angiogenesis, future validation of this specific finding in PAECs would be beneficial. Finally, our patient cohorts were not treatment-naïve. We sought to mitigate the potential confounding effects of treatment by intentionally focusing our discovery phase on patients in earlier NYHA functional classes, aiming to identify biomarkers present early in the disease process.


## Conclusions

In summary, we identified and validated a novel three-miRNA diagnostic signature (let-7a-5p, miR-9-5p, and miR-31-5p) that can effectively discriminate between PAH patients and healthy controls.. Our results highlight the downregulation of the tumor-suppressive let-7 family in PAH patients and the overexpression of miRNAs associated with proliferation (miR-9-5p and miR-31-5p). In a parallel mechanistic investigation, we identified miR-3168 as a novel upregulated in PAH patients’ plasma, Functionally, we showed that miR-3168 modulates BMPR2 protein levels and impair endothelial tube formation in vitro. Taken together, these findings not only provide a promising, clinically relevant biomarker panel but also implicate miR-3168 as a novel contributor to the endothelial dysfunction characteristic of PAH pathogenesis.
