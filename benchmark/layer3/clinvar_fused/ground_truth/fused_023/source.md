# Hypertrophy cardiomyopathy: A case of infantile-onset Pompe disease in cardiac magnetic resonance findings

## Abstract

There are two clinical types of Pompe disease: infantile-onset and late-onset, while the former is much more severe. We reported a typical case of Pompe disease in a 9-month infant who presenting with repeated pneumonia, growth retardation and hypomyotonia, hepatomegaly, accompany with elevated of serum creatine kinase and liver transaminase. Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) showed marked hypertrophy of both ventricles including the septum, and extensive myocardial late enhancement. The gene testing result was confirmed the disease finaly. Familiarity with the imaging findings can aid in making an accurate diagnosis and determining the appropriate treatment.


## Introduction

Pompe disease, also known as “Glycogen Storage Disease Type II” or “acid maltase deficiency”, is a rare autosomal recessive disease caused by mutation of acid alpha-glucosidase enzyme (GAA) gene, leading to progressive accumulation of glycogen in multiple organs, specially in heart, liver and skeletal muscles. However, there are few reports about CMR of Pompe disease. In this case report, we aimed to identify radiologic findings in patient of Pompe disease using CMR.

## Case presentation

A 9-month-old boy was born at term by a G1P1 mother and had a history of rescued. He was diagnosed as Down's syndrome when he was 4-month-old due to growth retardation and pneumonia in other hospital. He was sent to our hospital because of repeated high fever (up to 39.4 °C), accompany with progressive paroxysmal nonirritating cough, hoarseness and polypnea. Physical examination indicated he could hardly hold his head steadily and had abnormal facial characteristics include enlarged interorbital distances, small nasal bones, and protruding tongue. There was a grade 2/6 systolic ejection murmur as well as coarse breathing sounds bilaterally with fine rales. He had hypomyotonia and hepatomegaly as well.

Laboratory tests revealed elevated alanine aminotransferasc with 260 U/L (normal 9–50), aspartate aminotransferasc with 415 U/L (normal 15–40), creatine phosphokinase with 850 U/L (normal 20–173), creatine phosphokinase-MB with 76 U/L (normal 0–25), lactate dehydrogenase with 2317 U/L (normal 114–240), alpha-hydroxybutyric dehydrogenase with 2189 U/L (normal 76–250), brain natriuretic peptide with 3341.6 pg/ml (normal 0–100) and cardiac troponin I with 0.046 ng/ml (normal 0.000–0.033). Electrocardiogram (ECG) showed sinus tachycardia, left ventricular high voltage, counterclockwise rotation and ST-T changes. The echocardiogram demonstrated the left ventricular (LV) cavity was reduced in size due to marked LV and septum hypertrophy [LV posterior wall thickness of 20mm, LV septum thickness of 21mm with normal systolic function (left ventricular ejection function of 67%)] ( Fig. 1 ). Atrial septal defect was accidental noted. Both chest radiography and computed tomography (CT) suggested cardiomegaly and pneumonia. ( Fig. 2 , Fig. 3 ). Unenhanced CT showed hyperattenuation in the myocardium, similar to hemorrhage ( Fig. 3 ). The infant underwent CMR with a 3.0T equipment (Skyra, Siemens, German). The magnetic resonance scan protocols included half-Fourier acquisition single-shot turbo spin-echo, true fast imaging with steady-state free precession and phase-sensitive inversion recover with free-breathing and ECG gated. In addtion, real-time single-shot cine sequence instead of conventional segmented cine was used to suppress the motion artifacts due to sinus arrhythmia and free-breathing. There was prominent diffusive myocardial hypertrophy of bi-ventricles and papillaries, especially in LV and septum ( Fig. 4 ). Both left or right outflow tracts were not obstructed. ## Discussion

Infantile-onset Pompe disease typically presents with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and hypomyotonia, while there was few previous reports regarding the characteristics of CMR. In contrast to echocardiography, CMR can show not only cardiac structure and function, but myocardial tissue characteristics including myocardial fibrosis or deposition of anomalous substances like glycogen in this case. It is needed to differentiate Pompe disease from classic hypertrophy cardiomyopathy. Diffused biventricular hypertrophy with extensively LGE are the main aspects that distinguish it from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. However, we still have difficulties in distinguishing Pompe disease from other similar infiltrative cardiomyopathies like Danon disease, Fabry disease and amyloidosis by CMR only. But in fact, there are some subtle differences between the above-mentioned disease and Pompe disease in MRI. For example, LGE is mainly in the subendocardium in Danon disease [ 1 ]. In amyloidosis, LGE is diffuse throughout the bi-ventricular, particularly the subendocardium [ 2 ]. Besides, amyloidosis has normal-voltage or low-voltage QRS complexes because of accumulation of abnormal substances within myocardial interstitial rather than actual myocyte hypertrophy [ 3 ]. In Fabry's disease, LGE predominantly occurs in the basal segment of inferolateral LV wall [ 4 ].

The final diagnosis of Pompe disease relies on GAA gene test. However, in previous studies, the frequency of c.1935C > A mutation (p.Asp645Glu) was up to 80% among Chinese infantile patients [ 5 ], while the c.953T > C mutation (p.Met318Arg) has not been reported yet. The infants with Pompe disease would die of cardiorespiratory failure within their first year of life if without any treatment [ 6 ], while the enzyme replacement therapy is the only available treatment so far [ 7 ].

In summary, we described a case of classic infantile-onset Pompe disease with comprehensive examinations included clinical presentation, ECG, echocardiography, chest radiography, chest computed tomography, CMR and genetics test. Although the final diagnosis relies on genetic testing, diffuse biventricular hypertrophy and halo-like enhancement are its relatively typical characteristics on CMR, which plays an important role in differential diagnosis of hypertrophy cardiomyopathy of unknown reasons by combining with the clinical manifestations and laboratory tests. Familiarity with the radiologic findings of Pompe disease can aid in making an accurate diagnosis and treatment.
