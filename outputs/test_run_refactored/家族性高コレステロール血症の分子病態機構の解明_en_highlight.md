## 1. Introduction  
Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is characterized by elevated serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels from birth, tendon/skin xanthomas, and premature coronary artery disease. Additionally, LDL-C levels remain persistently high despite dietary modifications.  

The relationship between atherosclerosis and LDL-C has been established through the "LDL hypothesis," which posits that elevated LDL-C levels directly contribute to atherosclerotic progression. Over the past century, LDL-C has been recognized as critically important in atherosclerosis pathogenesis.  

FH is an autosomal dominant disorder with an estimated prevalence of 1% in the general population. The global prevalence of FH is approximately 1 in 300 individuals.  

FH patients typically exhibit LDL-C levels exceeding 370 mg/dL, with diagnostic criteria suggesting a prevalence of 1 in 36–100 individuals. However, 20–40% of FH patients do not meet the clinical diagnostic criteria, highlighting the need for further investigation into the molecular mechanisms and novel genetic factors underlying FH.  

## 2. Causative Genes of FH  
In 1973, Goldstein and Brown identified the *LDL receptor (LDLR)* gene as the primary cause of FH. To date, over 2,000 *LDLR* variants have been reported worldwide.  

*LDLR* pathogenic variants can be classified into defective-type (residual function: 2–25%) and negative-type (residual function: <2%), though many variants remain functionally uncharacterized.  

In 1987, the *apolipoprotein B (APOB)* gene was identified as the second causative gene of FH. *APOB* encodes apolipoprotein B-100 (*APOB*-100), the ligand for *LDLR*.  

*APOB* consists of five domains: three amphipathic α-helical domains and two amphipathic β-sheet domains (NH2-βα1-β1-α2-β2-α3-COOH). The β-sheet domains are critical for *APOB*-*LDLR* binding.  

The *APOB* p.(Arg3527Gln) variant reduces binding affinity between *APOB*-100 and *LDLR* but does not affect binding to other regions outside the *LDLR* binding domain (residues 3359–3369).  

FH caused by *APOB* variants generally presents with milder cardiovascular manifestations compared to *LDLR*-related FH.  

In Japan, only one family with FH caused by the *APOB* p.(Arg3527Gln) variant has been reported, indicating its rarity. Another variant, *APOB* p.(Arg3527Trp), has also been identified.  

In 2003, Abifadel et al. identified *proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9)* as the third causative gene of FH through linkage analysis.  

*PCSK9* consists of four domains: prodomain, catalytic domain, hinge region, and C-terminal domain. Gain-of-function *PCSK9* variants enhance *PCSK9*-mediated degradation of *LDLR*, reducing *LDLR* recycling to the cell surface.  

Normally, *LDLR* undergoes ~150 cycles of endocytosis and recycling. However, *PCSK9*-bound *LDLR* is not recycled and is degraded in lysosomes.  

One notable *PCSK9* pathogenic variant is p.(Asp374Tyr).

## 3. Genetic Characteristics of FH in Japan  
In Japan, genetic testing was performed on 1,550 probands clinically diagnosed with **familial hypercholesterolemia (FH)** or suspected FH, identifying pathogenic variants in **LDLR** and **PCSK9**[11]. Among 650 probands meeting clinical FH criteria, **LDLR** variants were detected in 57% (n=368), comprising 137 distinct pathogenic variants[12]. Using **ACMG** (American College of Medical Genetics) and **AMP** (Association for Molecular Pathology) guidelines, 92 variants were classified as pathogenic, while 2 remained uncertain[13,14].  

### Key Findings:  
- **LDLR**: Five recurrent variants accounted for 30.4% of cases, including:  
  - c.1845+2T>C (7.5%)  
  - p.(Cys338Ser) (6.4%)  
  - p.(Lys811*) (5.8%)  
  - p.(Asp433His) (5.5%)  
  - p.(Leu568Val) (5.2%)  
- **PCSK9**: 21 variants were identified in 7.8% (n=51) of probands. Four were pathogenic:  
  - p.(Glu32Lys) (88% of PCSK9 variants)  
  - p.(Asp129Asn)  
  - p.(Arg215His)  
  - p.(Arg496Trp) (Fig. 1)  

**Clinical Impact**:  
- Mean LDL-C levels: 275±73 mg/dL (**LDLR**) vs. 201±42 mg/dL (**PCSK9**).  
- Tendon xanthoma prevalence: 82% (**LDLR**) vs. 44% (**PCSK9**), indicating milder phenotypes with **PCSK9** variants[15].

## 4. Genetic Approaches to Unexplained FH  
Among 650 probands:  
- 64% had FH-causing variants in **LDLR**/**PCSK9**.  
- 36% had no identified variants ("unexplained FH").  

### Polygenic FH:  
- 10% of unexplained cases showed polygenic contributions via 12 SNPs (e.g., **APOE**, **SORT1**, **LDLRAP1**)[17].  
- **APOB** p.(Pro955Ser) was detected in 3.4% of controls vs. 15% in FH probands (OR=4.9, p=6.9×10⁻¹³)[Fig. 2].  

**Functional Evidence**:  
- p.(Pro955Ser) carriers had 44% lower LDL receptor binding affinity (Fig. 3).  
- Population prevalence: 0.6% (Japan), with strong GWAS associations[18,19].

## 5. Identifying Severe FH  
### Compound Heterozygosity:  
- Co-occurrence of **LDLR** pathogenic variants with:  
  - **PCSK9** p.(Glu32Lys) or p.(Val4Ile) (1.1–1.3% prevalence)[11,20–22].  
  - Variants in **ABCG5/8**, **APOE**, or **LDLRAP1**[23,24].  

**Research Gaps**:  
- Limited functional studies on combinatorial effects[25].

## 6. Conclusion  
Advances in genomics have clarified FH’s genetic diversity. **PCSK9** inhibitors now offer therapeutic options for high LDL-C, underscoring the need for comprehensive genetic profiling.
