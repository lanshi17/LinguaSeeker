## Abstract  
**Objective**: To analyze the clinical data of a child with familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) and generalized xanthomatosis and investigate its pathogenic gene mutations.  

**Methods**: Clinical data were collected from a child with FH and generalized xanthomatosis treated at Henan General Hospital on November 1, 2024. Peripheral blood samples from the proband and parents were obtained for genomic DNA extraction, with 100 healthy individuals as controls. High-throughput sequencing screened lipid metabolism-related genes in the proband to identify potential pathogenic mutations. PCR-Sanger sequencing validated the mutations, and pathogenicity was assessed using ACMG guidelines.  

**Results**: The proband presented with millet-to-soybean-sized yellowish maculopapular eruptions on bilateral upper eyelids, dorsal hand web spaces, knee extensors, postauricular areas, and the flexor side of the right middle finger metacarpophalangeal joint. Laboratory findings: normal liver/kidney function; total cholesterol 18.56 mmol/L, LDL-C 15.25 mmol/L, HDL-C 2.57 mmol/L, triglycerides 1.96 mmol/L, apoA-I 1.20 g/L, apoB 3.16 g/L. Histopathology showed diffuse foam cell infiltration around dermal vessels and appendages. Genetic testing revealed compound heterozygous LDLR mutations: c.763T>C (p.Cys255Arg) maternally inherited (exon 5) and c.941-12G>A (splicing) paternally inherited. Controls showed no LDLR mutations. The diagnosis was confirmed as FH with generalized xanthomatosis due to LDLR compound heterozygous mutations.  

**Conclusion**: The LDLR c.763T>C (p.Cys255Arg) and c.941-12G>A (splicing) compound heterozygous mutations likely caused FH with generalized xanthomatosis in this pedigree.  

**Keywords**: Familial hypercholesterolemia; Xanthomatosis; Low-density lipoprotein receptor gene; Compound heterozygous mutation

### Familial Hypercholesterolemia  
(**Familial Hypercholesterolemia**, **FH**) is a dominantly inherited lipid metabolism disorder primarily caused by autosomal dominant mutations. Typical manifestations include significantly elevated plasma low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (**LDL-C**), tendon xanthomas, xanthelasma, and premature coronary heart disease [[1]].  

The pathogenic genes involved in **FH** include:  
- **LDL receptor (LDLR)** gene (~90% of cases),  
- **Apolipoprotein B (Apo B)** gene,  
- **Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9)** gene, among others [[2]].  

Most **FH** cases result from single-gene mutations, though polygenic (20%–30%), environmental, or unknown genetic mutations may also contribute [[1]].  

**FH** inheritance patterns are categorized as:  
- **Heterozygous FH (HeFH)**: Prevalence 1/200–300,  
- **Homozygous FH (HoFH)**: Prevalence 1/170,000–300,000 [[1, 3–4]].  

Reports of **compound heterozygous mutations** causing **FH with generalized xanthomatosis** are rare. This study analyzes clinical data from a pediatric case of **FH with generalized xanthomatosis**, investigates its pathogenic gene mutations, and reports the findings below.

## 3 Discussion
LDLR is a 160 kDa transmembrane cell surface protein mediating LDL-C hepatic uptake and degradation[7-8]. Located at 19p13.2-13.3, the 45 kb LDLR gene contains 18 exons encoding 839 amino acids[2,9-10]. Since Goldstein and Brown first described LDLR mutations disrupting cholesterol metabolism in 1974, over 2,200 LDLR mutations have been identified, including nonsense, missense, splicing, insertions/deletions, and large rearrangements[11-14]. Exon 4 (ligand-binding domain) mutations are most common[15].  

LDLR mutations are classified into 5 functional categories:  
1. Complete synthesis failure  
2. Impaired intracellular transport (accounts for >50% FH cases)  
3. Defective LDL binding  
4. Impaired clustering in clathrin-coated pits  
5. Failed recycling[2,7]  

LDLR mutations show significant genetic heterogeneity. Phenotypic variability stems from genetic drift, founder effects, and consanguinity[2]. Mutation type/location affects LDLR synthesis, maturation, and function, determining FH severity (e.g., nonsense mutations cause most severe phenotypes)[13]. Genotype-phenotype severity follows:  
LDLR null homozygotes > compound heterozygotes (defective + null) > LDLRAP1/LDLR defective homozygotes > ApoB/PCSK9 gain-of-function homozygotes > double heterozygotes > HeFH[16].  

FH exhibits ethnic-specific mutation spectra:  
- Mexico: p.Leu759Serfs*6 most common[18]  
- Netherlands: 66.5% cases attributable to 9 recurrent mutations[9]  
- Finland: 75% HeFH from 4 characteristic mutations[19]  
- Canada: 63% FH from one large deletion[20]  
- Latinos: p.Asp360His nearly exclusive[18]  
- Tunisia: Exon 10 p.Ser493ArgfsX44 prevalent[21]

### Common Exon 4 Hotspot Mutations  
The hotspot mutations in **exon 4** differ, suggesting that the Chinese population may harbor a broader mutation spectrum.  

In addition to the classic Mendelian monogenic inheritance pattern, **Familial Hypercholesterolemia (FH)** has also been reported to exhibit polygenic synergy, single-gene multi-locus compound mutations, and other genetic patterns.  

- **Suppressa et al.** [[23]] reported a 28-year-old female **FH** patient who carried both a homozygous **LDLR** gene mutation and a heterozygous **PCSK9** gene mutation, presenting with typical **HoFH** clinical phenotypes, including coronary and non-coronary atherosclerosis. She underwent three coronary stent implantations, one coronary artery bypass graft, and aortic/mitral valve replacements. Her **LDL-C** was controlled and reduced to **1.16 mmol/L** after treatment with lomitapide combined with statins, ezetimibe, and evolocumab.  
- **Ma et al.** [[24]] reported a 10-year-old Chinese boy carrying a maternally inherited **LDLR** pathogenic mutation **[p.(His583Tyr)]** and a de novo mutation **[p.(Gln242*)]**. The patient exhibited generalized cutaneous xanthomas and tendon xanthomas with elevated **LDL-C** levels (**14.87 mmol/L**). After treatment with simvastatin (**40 mg/d**) and ezetimibe (**10 mg/d**), his **LDL-C** levels decreased by **57%**.  
- **Rabacchi et al.** [[25]] reported an **HoFH** family in which the probands were a pair of 24-year-old monozygotic twin sisters carrying two **LDLR** pathogenic mutations: a maternally inherited missense mutation **[p.(G335S)]** and a paternally inherited **24 bp deletion** at the exon 8-intron 8 junction. Their mother and two siblings were carriers of a heterozygous **ApoB** missense mutation **[p.(S2429T)]**.  
- **Alnouri et al.** [[26]] reported a 45-year-old female **FH** patient with a homozygous **LDLR** mutation **[c.1255T>G, p.(Tyr419Asp)]** and a heterozygous **LDLRAP1** mutation **[c.604_605delTCinsA, p.(Ser202Thrfs*2)]**. Family analysis revealed two inheritance patterns among her six children: three carried dual heterozygous **LDLR/LDLRAP1** mutations, while the other three carried only heterozygous **LDLR** mutations.  
- **Al-Allaf et al.** [[27]] reported a case of compound heterozygous **FH**, carrying a maternally inherited **LDLR** exon 12 nonsense mutation **[p.(Trp577Cys)]** and a paternally inherited **LDLR** exon 14 frameshift mutation **[p.(Gly676Alafs*33)]**, suggesting a biallelic inheritance pattern in **FH**.  

In this study, the proband carried both a maternally inherited **LDLR** exon 5 missense mutation **[c.763T>C, p.Cys255Arg]** and a paternally inherited **LDLR** splicing mutation **[c.941-12G>A]**, consistent with previously reported compound heterozygous **FH** cases. The paternally inherited **c.941-12G>A** splicing mutation is rare and classified as a **"variant of uncertain significance (VUS)"**, yet it enriches the **LDLR** pathogenic mutation spectrum.

### Clinical Manifestations  
**FH** often leads to cholesterol deposition in the skin, causing **xanthomatosis** (incidence <15%), frequently affecting tendons (elbows, hands, Achilles), periorbital regions, and superior cornea [[1, 28]].  

In this study:  
- The proband’s skin lesions were pathologically confirmed as **generalized xanthomatosis**.  
- Serum total cholesterol (**18.56 mmol/L**) and **LDL-C** (**15.25 mmol/L**) were markedly elevated, indicating dyslipidemia.  
- Genetic sequencing revealed heterozygous **LDLR** mutations: **[c.763T>C]** (classified as **"likely pathogenic"**) and **[c.941-12G>A]** (**VUS**).  
- Based on clinical presentation, family history, lab tests, histopathology, and genetic results, the proband was diagnosed with **FH** and **generalized xanthomatosis** due to **compound heterozygous LDLR mutations**.

### Treatment Insights  
- Low-to-moderate statin doses are safe and effective for pediatric **FH**, aiding achievement of treatment targets (**LDL-C <3.49 mmol/L**) [[29]].  
- **LDLR** mutation types influence **LDL-C** levels and statin response [[30]].  
- **Evolocumab** (a PCSK9-targeting monoclonal antibody) effectively reduces **LDL-C** and other lipids in **HeFH** children with good safety [[31]].  
- In this study, rosuvastatin calcium (**10 mg/d**) significantly improved lipids but failed to achieve **LDL-C** targets. **Evolocumab** may be a future option upon meeting age requirements.
