**Abstract**  
**Objective:** To analyze the clinical data of a child with familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) and generalized xanthomatosis and investigate its pathogenic gene mutations.  
**Methods:** Clinical data were collected from a child with FH and generalized xanthomatosis treated at Henan General Hospital on November 1, 2024. Peripheral blood samples were obtained from the proband and parents for genomic DNA extraction, with 100 healthy individuals as controls. High-throughput sequencing was performed to screen lipid metabolism-related genes in the proband to identify potential pathogenic mutations. PCR-Sanger sequencing validated the mutations, and pathogenicity was assessed using ACMG guidelines.  
**Results:** The proband presented with millet-to-soybean-sized yellowish macules/papules on medial upper eyelids, dorsal hand web spaces, knee extensors, postauricular regions, and flexor aspect of the right middle finger metacarpophalangeal joint. Laboratory tests showed normal hepatic/renal function but markedly abnormal lipid profile: total cholesterol 18.56 mmol/L, LDL-C 15.25 mmol/L, HDL-C 2.57 mmol/L, triglycerides 1.96 mmol/L, apoA-I 1.20 g/L, apoB 3.16 g/L. Histopathology revealed diffuse foam cell infiltration around dermal vasculature and adnexa. Genetic testing identified compound heterozygous mutations in LDLR gene: c.763T>C (maternally inherited, exon 5, causing p.Cys255Arg) and c.941-12G>A (paternally inherited splicing variant). Healthy controls showed no LDLR mutations. The diagnosis was confirmed as FH with generalized xanthomatosis caused by compound heterozygous LDLR mutations.  
**Conclusion:** The compound heterozygous LDLR mutations c.763T>C (p.Cys255Arg) and c.941-12G>A (splicing) are likely pathogenic for this FH case with generalized xanthomatosis.

## Introduction  
Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is an autosomal dominant lipid metabolism disorder characterized by elevated plasma LDL-C, tendon xanthomas, xanthelasma, and premature coronary artery disease[1]. Over 90% of FH cases involve LDLR gene mutations[2]. We report a rare case of FH with generalized xanthomatosis caused by compound heterozygous LDLR mutations.  

## Materials and Methods  
### 1.1 Clinical Data  
An 11-year-old boy presented with generalized yellowish skin lesions for 2 years. Physical examination revealed multiple xanthomas (Figure 2) and surgical scars. Lipid panel showed severe dyslipidemia (Table 1). Postauricular biopsy demonstrated foam cell infiltration (Figure 3).  

### 1.2 Genetic Analysis  
Whole-exome sequencing identified LDLR mutations validated by Sanger sequencing. Pathogenicity was classified per ACMG standards[6].  

## Results  
### 2.1 Genetic Findings  
Compound heterozygous LDLR mutations:  
- c.763T>C (p.Cys255Arg): PM1+PM2+PP3+PP4 (Likely Pathogenic)  
- **c.941-12G>A: PM2+PM3+PP4 (VUS)**  

### 2.2 Treatment  
Rosuvastatin (10 mg/day) reduced LDL-C by 40.9% after 3 months.  

## Discussion  
This represents a novel case of FH with generalized xanthomatosis caused by compound heterozygous LDLR mutations, expanding the mutational spectrum. Early statin therapy effectively improved lipid profiles but did not achieve target LDL-C levels, suggesting potential need for PCSK9 inhibitors in future management.
