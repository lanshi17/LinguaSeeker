## 1. Introduction  
Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is characterized by elevated serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels from birth, tendon/skin xanthomas, and premature coronary artery disease. Additionally, LDL-C levels remain persistently high despite dietary modifications. The relationship between atherosclerosis and LDL-C has been established through epidemiological studies, and the "LDL hypothesis" suggests that elevated LDL-C levels directly contribute to atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. FH is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, with an estimated prevalence of 1% in the general population. The global prevalence of FH is approximately 1 in 300 individuals.  

FH patients typically exhibit LDL-C levels exceeding 370 mg/dL, with an estimated incidence of 1 in 36–100 individuals in certain populations. However, 20–40% of FH patients do not meet the diagnostic criteria for heterozygous FH, suggesting phenotypic variability. This review focuses on the molecular pathophysiology of FH and explores novel genetic mechanisms underlying FH.  

## 2. Causative Genes of FH  
In 1973, Goldstein and Brown identified the LDL receptor (LDLR) gene as the causative gene for FH. To date, over 2,000 LDLR variants have been reported worldwide. LDLR mutations can be classified into defective-type (residual function: 2–25%) and negative-type (complete loss of function: <2%) mutations, with many variants remaining functionally uncharacterized.  

In 1987, the second causative gene for FH, apolipoprotein B (APOB), was identified. APOB encodes the ligand for LDLR, APOB-100. APOB-100 consists of five domains: three amphipathic α-helical domains and two amphipathic β-sheet domains (NH2-βα1-β1-α2-β2-α3-COOH). The β-sheet domains mediate binding to LDLR. The most common APOB variant, p.(Arg3527Gln), reduces the binding affinity of APOB-100 to LDLR but does not affect other LDLR-binding regions (residues 3359–3369). FH caused by APOB mutations typically presents with milder cardiovascular manifestations compared to LDLR-related FH.  

In Japan, only one family with FH caused by the APOB p.(Arg3527Gln) variant has been reported, indicating its rarity. Other APOB variants, such as p.(Arg3527Trp), have also been reported.  

In 2003, Abifadel et al. identified proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) as the third causative gene for FH through linkage analysis. PCSK9 consists of four domains: a prodomain, catalytic domain, hinge region, and C-terminal domain. Gain-of-function PCSK9 variants increase LDLR degradation, reducing LDLR recycling to the cell surface. While wild-type LDLR undergoes ~150 cycles of endocytosis and recycling, PCSK9-bound LDLR is targeted for lysosomal degradation. The most common PCSK9 variant, p.(Asp374Tyr), is associated with FH.

## 3. Genetic Characteristics of FH in Japan  
In Japan, 1,550 patients clinically diagnosed with **familial hypercholesterolemia (FH)** or suspected FH were screened for variants in **LDLR**, **APOB**, and **PCSK9**. ## 6. Conclusion  
Advances in genomics have clarified FH’s genetic diversity. **PCSK9 inhibitors**, developed through FH research, now effectively manage elevated LDL-C in FH and broader populations.
