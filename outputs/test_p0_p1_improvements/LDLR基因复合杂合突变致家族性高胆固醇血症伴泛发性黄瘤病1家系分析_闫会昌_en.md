### Familial Hypercholesterolemia  
(**Familial Hypercholesterolemia**, **FH**) is a dominantly inherited lipid metabolism disorder characterized by significantly elevated plasma levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (**LDL-C**), tendon xanthomas, xanthelasma, and premature coronary heart disease[1].  

The pathogenic genes involved in **FH** include:  
- **Low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR)** gene (~90%),  
- **Apolipoprotein B (Apo B)** gene,  
- **Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9)** gene, among others[2].  

Most **FH** cases result from single-gene mutations, though polygenic (20%–30%), environmental, or unknown genetic mutations may also contribute[1].  

**FH** inheritance patterns are classified as:  
- **Heterozygous FH (HeFH)**: Prevalence 1/200–300,  
- **Homozygous FH (HoFH)**: Prevalence 1/170,000–300,000[1,3–4].  

Reports of **compound heterozygous mutations** causing **FH with generalized xanthomatosis** are rare. This study analyzes clinical data from a pediatric case of **FH with generalized xanthomatosis** and explores its genetic mutations.

### Key Findings  

The hotspot mutations in **exon 4** differ, suggesting that the Chinese population may harbor a broader mutation spectrum.  

In addition to the classic Mendelian monogenic inheritance pattern, **Familial Hypercholesterolemia (FH)** has also been reported to exhibit polygenic synergy and monogenic multi-locus compound mutation inheritance patterns.  

- **Suppressa et al.** [[23]] reported a 28-year-old female FH patient who carried both a homozygous **LDLR** mutation and a heterozygous **PCSK9** mutation, presenting with typical **HoFH** clinical phenotypes, including coronary and non-coronary atherosclerosis. She underwent three coronary stent implantations, one coronary artery bypass graft, and aortic/mitral valve replacements. Her **LDL-C** was later controlled and reduced to **1.16 mmol/L** with lomitapide combined with statins, ezetimibe, and evolocumab.  
- **Ma et al.** [[24]] described a 10-year-old Chinese boy carrying a maternally inherited **LDLR** pathogenic mutation **[p.(His583Tyr)]** and a de novo mutation **[p.(Gln242*)]**. The patient exhibited generalized cutaneous xanthomas and tendon xanthomas with elevated **LDL-C (14.87 mmol/L)**. After treatment with simvastatin (**40 mg/d**) and ezetimibe (**10 mg/d**), his **LDL-C** decreased by **57%**.  
- **Rabacchi et al.** [[25]] reported an **HoFH** family in which the probands were 24-year-old monozygotic twin sisters carrying two **LDLR** mutations: a maternally inherited missense mutation **[p.(G335S)]** and a paternally inherited **24 bp deletion** at the exon 8-intron 8 junction. Their mother and two siblings were carriers of an **ApoB** heterozygous missense mutation **[p.(S2429T)]**.  
- **Alnouri et al.** [[26]] documented a 45-year-old female FH patient with both a homozygous **LDLR** mutation **[c.1255T>G, p.(Tyr419Asp)]** and a heterozygous **LDLRAP1** mutation **[c.604_605delTCinsA, p.(Ser202Thrfs*2)]**. Family analysis revealed two inheritance patterns among her six children: three carried dual heterozygous **LDLR/LDLRAP1** mutations, while the other three carried only heterozygous **LDLR** mutations.  
- **Al-Allaf et al.** [[27]] described a case of compound heterozygous FH carrying a maternally inherited **LDLR** nonsense mutation **[p.(Trp577Cys)]** in **exon 12** and a paternally inherited **LDLR** frameshift mutation **[p.(Gly676Alafs*33)]** in **exon 14**, suggesting a biallelic inheritance pattern in FH.  

In this study, the proband carried both a maternally inherited **LDLR** missense mutation **[c.763T>C, p.Cys255Arg]** in **exon 5** and a paternally inherited **LDLR** splicing mutation **[c.941-12G>A]**, consistent with previously reported compound heterozygous FH cases. The splicing mutation **[c.941-12G>A]** is rare and classified as a **"variant of uncertain significance (VUS)"**, but it enriches the **LDLR** pathogenic mutation spectrum.  

### Clinical Manifestations  
FH often leads to cholesterol deposition in the skin, causing **xanthomatosis** (incidence **<15%**), commonly affecting the elbow tendons, hand tendons, Achilles tendon, periorbital region, and superior cornea [[1, 28]].  

In this study:  
- The proband’s skin lesions were pathologically confirmed as **generalized xanthomatosis**.  
- Serum total cholesterol (**18.56 mmol/L**) and **LDL-C (15.25 mmol/L)** were markedly elevated, indicating dyslipidemia.  
- Genetic sequencing revealed heterozygous **LDLR** mutations: **[c.763T>C]** (classified as **"likely pathogenic"**) and **[c.941-12G>A]** (**VUS**).  
- Based on clinical presentation, family history, lab tests, histopathology, and genetic results, the proband was diagnosed with **FH and generalized xanthomatosis due to compound heterozygous LDLR mutations**.  

### Treatment Insights  
- Low-to-moderate statin doses are safe and effective for pediatric FH, aiding in achieving the treatment target of **LDL-C <3.49 mmol/L** [[29]].  
- **LDLR** mutation types influence **LDL-C** levels and statin response [[30]].  
- **Evolocumab** (a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting **PCSK9**) effectively reduces **LDL-C** and other lipid parameters in **HeFH** children with good safety [[31]].  
- In this study, rosuvastatin calcium (**10 mg/d**) significantly improved lipid levels but failed to achieve **LDL-C** targets. **Evolocumab** may be a future option once age requirements are met.  

### Limitations  
- Single-family analysis with a small sample size limits generalizability.  
- Lack of in vitro validation for **LDLR** protein functional impact.  
- Absence of long-term follow-up data for the proband and family members.  
- Further studies with larger cohorts are needed.
