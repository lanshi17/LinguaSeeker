## 1. Introduction  
Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is characterized by elevated serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels from birth, tendon/skin xanthomas, and premature coronary artery disease. Additionally, LDL-C levels remain persistently high despite dietary modifications.  

The relationship between atherosclerosis and LDL-C has been established through the "LDL hypothesis," which posits that elevated LDL-C levels directly contribute to atherosclerotic progression. Over the past century, LDL-C has been recognized as critically important in atherosclerosis pathogenesis. FH is an autosomal dominant disorder, with an estimated prevalence of 1 in 200–500 individuals in the general population. Globally, FH affects approximately 1 in 300 individuals.  

FH patients typically exhibit LDL-C levels exceeding 370 mg/dL, with prevalence estimates ranging from 1 in 36 to 1 in 100 individuals in certain populations. However, 20–40% of FH patients do not meet the diagnostic criteria for heterozygous FH, suggesting phenotypic variability. This review discusses the molecular mechanisms underlying FH and recent advances in genetic analysis.  

## 2. Causative Genes of FH  
In 1973, Goldstein and Brown identified the *LDL receptor (LDLR)* gene as the causative gene for FH. To date, over 2,000 *LDLR* variants have been reported worldwide. *LDLR* mutations can be classified into defective-type (2–25% residual function) and negative-type (<2% residual function) mutations, though many variants remain functionally uncharacterized.  

In 1987, the *apolipoprotein B (APOB)* gene was identified as the second causative gene for FH. *APOB* encodes apolipoprotein B-100 (*APOB*-100), the ligand for *LDLR*. *APOB*-100 consists of five structural domains: three amphipathic α-helical domains and two amphipathic β-sheet domains (NH2-βα1-β1-α2-β2-α3-COOH). The β-sheet domains are critical for *LDLR* binding. The *APOB* p.(Arg3527Gln) variant reduces binding affinity between *APOB*-100 and *LDLR* but does not affect interactions outside the *LDLR*-binding region (residues 3359–3369). FH caused by *APOB* mutations generally presents with milder cardiovascular manifestations compared to *LDLR*-related FH.  

As of 2020, only one Japanese family with FH caused by the *APOB* p.(Arg3527Gln) variant has been reported, indicating its rarity in Japan. Other *APOB* variants, such as p.(Arg3527Trp), have also been reported.  

In 2003, Abifadel et al. identified *proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9)* as the third causative gene for FH through linkage analysis. *PCSK9* consists of four domains: a prodomain, catalytic domain, hinge region, and C-terminal domain. Gain-of-function *PCSK9* variants enhance *PCSK9*-mediated degradation of *LDLR*. While *LDLR* normally recycles to the cell surface approximately 150 times, *PCSK9*-bound *LDLR* is diverted to lysosomal degradation instead of recycling. The *PCSK9* p.(Asp374Tyr) variant is one such gain-of-function mutation.

## 3. Genetic Characteristics of FH in Japan  
In a nationwide cohort study of 1,550 clinically diagnosed FH patients, pathogenic variants in **LDLR** and **PCSK9** were identified. Among 650 probands meeting Dutch Lipid Clinic Network (DLCN) criteria, **LDLR** variants (137 distinct types) were detected in 57% (n=368). These were classified using **ACMG/AMP guidelines**, with 92 variants designated pathogenic/likely pathogenic and 2 as benign.  

Notably:  
- Five **LDLR** variants accounted for 30.4% of cases, including:  
  - c.1845+2T>C (7.5%)  
  - p.(Cys338Ser) (6.4%)  
  - p.(Lys811*) (5.8%)  
  - p.(Asp433His) (5.5%)  
  - p.(Leu568Val) (5.2%)  
- **p.(Lys811*)** alone represented 41% of pathogenic **LDLR** variants in one cohort.  

### PCSK9 Variants  
- 21 variants detected in 7.8% (n=51) of probands.  
- Four classified pathogenic:  
  - p.(Glu32Lys) (most prevalent at 88%)  
  - p.(Asp129Asn)  
  - p.(Arg215His)  
  - p.(Arg496Trp) (**Fig. 1**)  
- **p.(Glu32Lys)** carriers showed milder phenotypes (mean LDL-C 201±42 mg/dL vs. 275±73 mg/dL for **LDLR** variants) and lower tendon xanthoma prevalence (44% vs. 82%).

## 4. Genetic Approach to FH of Unknown Etiology  
Among 650 DLCN-diagnosed probands:  
- 64% had FH-causing variants in **LDLR/PCSK9**  
- 36% remained genetically undefined ("probable FH")  

Polygenic risk scores (PRS) analysis of 22 SNPs implicated **LDLR** locus contributions. In 122 Japanese FH cases:  
- **APOB p.(Pro955Ser)** was identified in 9.8% (n=63; OR=4.9, p=6.9×10⁻¹³) (**Fig. 2**)  
- Carriers showed defective LDL binding (44% reduction vs. controls) (**Fig. 3**)  
- Population frequency: 0.6% (vs. 15% in FH patients)

## 5. Identification of Severe FH  
Compound heterozygosity cases:  
- **LDLR** variant + **PCSK9 p.(Glu32Lys)**: Enhanced LDL-C elevation  
- **LDLR** variant + **PCSK9 p.(Val4Ile)**: 40% increased LDL-C  
Other modifier genes: **ABCG5/8**, **APOE**, **LDLRAP1**

## 6. Conclusion  
Advances in genetic analysis have clarified FH's molecular heterogeneity. **PCSK9 inhibitors** now offer targeted therapy for high LDL-C in FH.
